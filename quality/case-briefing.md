# Case briefing field guide: doctrine without invented certainty

A useful case brief answers: **who sued whom; for what; at which procedural stage; under what source of law; what precise question; what holding; why; what did the court expressly reserve; and what changed afterward?**

## Standard seven-part brief
1. **Identity:** *Case*, reporter/docket, court, date; link to official primary opinion.
2. **Relevant facts:** Exclude irrelevant drama; include facts the holding needs.
3. **Procedural path:** Distinguish trial ruling, intermediate appeal, certiorari, reversal, remand.
4. **Issue:** Formulate a yes/no legal question constrained by facts.
5. **Rule and holding:** Quote only brief necessary wording; provide majority-opinion pinpoint.
6. **Rationale and opposition:** Distinguish majority, concurrence, dissent and alternative legal interpretations.
7. **Scope / application:** Identify facts that would change the result, subsequent treatment unknowns, and an original startup hypothetical.

## Annotated example — *Coinbase v. Suski*
**Source:** [Supreme Court official slip opinion, No. 23-3 (May 23, 2024)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-3_879d.pdf). [Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/23-3.html).  
**Facts:** Account arbitration/delegation provision and promotion's California forum clause pointed in different directions.  
**Procedure:** District court refused to compel arbitration; Ninth Circuit affirmed; Supreme Court affirmed.  
**Issue:** Which decision maker resolves the conflict over *which agreement governs arbitrability*?  
**Holding:** The **court**, not the arbitrator, decides the antecedent contract conflict. **Majority slip pp. 4–9.**  
**Rationale:** Arbitration is contractual; a delegation clause in one disputed contract does not by itself answer which of two competing contracts controls this very allocation.  
**Opposing/other writing:** Justice Gorsuch concurred; **not a dissent**. The Court was unanimous on the judgment.  
**Limit:** Neither “courts always decide every arbitrability dispute” nor “the most recent agreement always governs” follows from this opinion.  
**Startup exercise:** A standard SaaS MSA delegates disputes to arbitration; a later signed event-specific contract names state court. Identify integration clauses, parties, scope, notice and forum language. Explain threshold determination separately from ultimate merits.

## Annotated example — *Google v. Oracle*
**Source:** [Supreme Court slip opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/18-956_new_0e04.pdf).  
**Facts:** Some Java API declarations copied to facilitate programmer interoperability on Android.  
**Issue:** Whether that particular copying is fair use, assuming copyrightability.  
**Holding:** Yes, on facts evaluated under 17 U.S.C. § 107.  
**Opposition:** Thomas dissent joined by Alito.  
**Limit:** The Court did **not** decide whether the declarations are copyrightable and did not decide blanket legality of LLM training or third-party asset copying.  
**Application question:** How does the functional role of declarations differ from the expressive role of a game sprite?

## How to test a brief
A grader should reject these statements:
- “Everalbum establishes a Supreme Court precedent.” (**FTC consent proceeding**)
- “The 2024 click-to-cancel FTC amendment is currently binding.” (**vacated; 2026 conforming action**)
- “A Supreme Court syllabus is part of the opinion.” (**not binding majority text**)
- “GPL applies to all SaaS code without regard to conveyance, modifications or network requirements.” (**actual license and fact analysis necessary**)
- “A 2026 official consolidated EU Act automatically makes each obligation operative in 2026.” (**application and transition dates differ**)
