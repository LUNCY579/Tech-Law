# Lesson 03 — Corporate formation, equity and securities

**Goal:** Distinguish entity formation, ownership, financing instruments and securities compliance.

## 1. Entity versus product
An LLC and a corporation are different legal structures with different governance and tax consequences. “Delaware C-corp” describes both incorporation under Delaware law and an intended federal tax treatment; do not assume a tax election and incorporation are the same operation.

[Law] Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL) Title 8 controls core corporate mechanics. See §102 (certificate of incorporation), §141(a) (board manages business and affairs, subject to exceptions), §152 (stock issuance consideration), and §251 (mergers). [Official Delaware code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/).

## 2. Founder stock and vesting
Illustration: two founders receive a combined 8,000,000 shares, with a separately reserved 2,000,000-share option pool. The fully diluted illustrative denominator is 10,000,000; founders collectively hold 80% on that assumption. **This is not an incorporation or tax instruction.** Founder restricted stock, repurchase rights and vesting need documented agreements. Consider the federal 83(b) election deadline under applicable tax rules for restricted property; ask tax counsel before issuance. [26 U.S.C. §83](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/26/83).

## 3. What a SAFE does not guarantee
A SAFE is a contractual right with conversion rules, not a simple promise of a fixed percentage in every scenario. Pre-money versus post-money mechanics, valuation caps, discounts, later financing and option-pool expansion can affect dilution. Work from the **actual executed instrument**.

## 4. Raising money triggers securities law
[Law] Offers and sales of securities generally require registration or a valid exemption. Securities Act of 1933 §5, 15 U.S.C. §77e. [Official text](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/77e).

The SEC's [Exempt Offerings guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings) explains Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c). Importantly, general solicitation rules differ: advertising a financing publicly may foreclose an otherwise contemplated private-offering path. Rule 506(c) requires accredited-investor purchasers and reasonable verification steps; don't use “they said they are accredited” as a substitute.

## 5. Real judicial case: fiduciary process
*Smith v. Van Gorkom*, 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985), is a landmark Delaware Supreme Court decision regarding directors' duty of care in approving a merger. The precise holding and subsequent DGCL §102(b)(7) exculpation developments should be studied together rather than generalized to all founder decisions. [Delaware judicial opinions portal](https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/) [official opinions index].

## Startup hypothetical
A founder publicly posts “Invest $5k for equity, guaranteed to double” before consulting securities counsel. Flag (1) an offer of securities, (2) exemption and solicitation risk, and (3) materially misleading performance claims.

## Exercise
Model a financing: 10M fully diluted shares; a new issuance of 2M shares to an investor. What fraction does the investor hold immediately after, assuming no other changes? **Answer:** 2/12 = 16.67%. This is arithmetic, not a SAFE-conversion forecast.

## Advanced seminar — founder finance and governance

### A. Formation is a sequence, not a checkbox
1. Select entity after comparing tax, investor expectations and governance.
2. Verify name and file the formation document under applicable state law.
3. Approve organizational actions and share issuance through authorized corporate processes.
4. Execute IP assignment, founder restricted-stock and vesting agreements where applicable.
5. Create a cap table and track authorized, issued and reserved shares separately.
6. Review federal tax filing duties, employment classification and any relevant 83(b) election deadline with qualified advisers.

**Example:** Authorized shares = 12M; issued to founders = 8M; unissued option-plan reserve = 2M; unallocated = 2M. Authorized capacity is not identical to currently outstanding shares. Fully diluted capitalization requires explicit assumptions.

### B. Securities exemptions are legal gates
For a funding plan, document (i) issuer/entity, (ii) instrument, (iii) intended purchasers, (iv) outreach/solicitation plan, (v) exemption and exact requirements, (vi) filings and state notice obligations. Under the SEC's official description, Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c) differ materially on general solicitation and investor verification. Do not start advertising a private offering while exemption selection is unresolved.

### C. Board duty case clinic
*Smith v. Van Gorkom*, 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985): analyze process quality and informed decisionmaking rather than treating it as a rule that every bad commercial outcome establishes a fiduciary breach. Review DGCL §102(b)(7) and current Delaware law before generalizing the result; the course has not independently authenticated an official full-text copy of this historic opinion.

### D. Cap table drill
Founders hold 8M shares, option reserve is 2M (10M fully diluted before financing). New investor receives 2M newly issued shares, with no pool top-up. **Investor fraction:** 2/12 = 16.67%; **founder fraction:** 8/12 = 66.67%; **pool fraction:** 2/12 = 16.67%, on the stated fully diluted assumptions. If 2M instead were transferred from founders, total share count and dilution would differ. Explain why this is not a SAFE conversion formula.
