# Lesson 03 — Corporate formation, equity and securities

**Goal:** Distinguish entity formation, ownership, financing instruments and securities compliance.

## 1. Entity versus product
An LLC and a corporation are different legal structures with different governance and tax consequences. “Delaware C-corp” describes both incorporation under Delaware law and an intended federal tax treatment; do not assume a tax election and incorporation are the same operation.

[Law] Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL) Title 8 controls core corporate mechanics. See §102 (certificate of incorporation), §141(a) (board manages business and affairs, subject to exceptions), §152 (stock issuance consideration), and §251 (mergers). [Official Delaware code](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/).

## 2. Founder stock and vesting
Illustration: two founders receive a combined 8,000,000 shares, with a separately reserved 2,000,000-share option pool. The fully diluted illustrative denominator is 10,000,000; founders collectively hold 80% on that assumption. **This is not an incorporation or tax instruction.** Founder restricted stock, repurchase rights and vesting need documented agreements. Consider the federal 83(b) election deadline under applicable tax rules for restricted property; ask tax counsel before issuance. [26 U.S.C. §83](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title26-section83&num=0&edition=prelim).

## 3. What a SAFE does not guarantee
A SAFE is a contractual right with conversion rules, not a simple promise of a fixed percentage in every scenario. Pre-money versus post-money mechanics, valuation caps, discounts, later financing and option-pool expansion can affect dilution. Work from the **actual executed instrument**.

## 4. Raising money triggers securities law
[Law] Offers and sales of securities generally require registration or a valid exemption. Securities Act of 1933 §5, 15 U.S.C. §77e. [Official text](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title15-section77e&num=0&edition=prelim).

The SEC's [Exempt Offerings guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings) explains Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c). Importantly, general solicitation rules differ: advertising a financing publicly may foreclose an otherwise contemplated private-offering path. Rule 506(c) requires accredited-investor purchasers and reasonable verification steps; don't use “they said they are accredited” as a substitute.

## 5. Real judicial case: fiduciary process
*Smith v. Van Gorkom*, 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985), is a landmark Delaware Supreme Court decision regarding directors' duty of care in approving a merger. The precise holding and subsequent DGCL §102(b)(7) exculpation developments should be studied together rather than generalized to all founder decisions. [Delaware judicial opinions portal](https://courts.delaware.gov/opinions/) [official opinions index].

## Startup hypothetical
A founder publicly posts “Invest $5k for equity, guaranteed to double” before consulting securities counsel. Flag (1) an offer of securities, (2) exemption and solicitation risk, and (3) materially misleading performance claims.

## Exercise
Model a financing: 10M fully diluted shares; a new issuance of 2M shares to an investor. What fraction does the investor hold immediately after, assuming no other changes? **Answer:** 2/12 = 16.67%. This is arithmetic, not a SAFE-conversion forecast.
