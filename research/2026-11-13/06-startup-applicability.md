# Applied startup legal worksheet: from legal text to evidence

These exercises are hypothetical; no legal accreditation, employment-assessment validation or regulatory certification is implied.

## Required fact sheet for every feature

Record (1) system and version; (2) customers, residents and countries; (3) actor and counterparty roles; (4) data collected and purpose; (5) use in consequential decisions; (6) operative law and exact source version; (7) thresholds, exclusions, dates and transitions; (8) remedies/enforcement; (9) technical controls and evidence; (10) unknown facts; (11) reviewer and next check date.

## Case A: job-assessment credential and employer dashboard

A company administers an AI-assisted skill test, generates a signed result, issues a public verification URL and sells applicant rankings to employers. Analyze Title VII/ADA and use-specific test validation; FCRA consumer reporting possibilities; Illinois employment AI, NYC AEDT, Colorado ADMT and EU Annex III according to actual locations and roles. A cryptographic signature proves neither test accuracy nor current validity, and neither establishes approval by an external credentialing body.

**Evidence to retain:** job analysis and assessment construct; cutoff justification; accommodations; versioned adverse-impact assessment with privacy safeguards; vendor/role contract; assessment scoring and model lineage; issuer signing-key history; revocation and expiry semantics; claims review; candidate challenge path. Label proprietary pilot assessments as proprietary pilot assessments.

## Case B: compact language-model research and licensing

A startup trains a small model on datasets, releases adapters and offers an API. Evaluate separate copyright and dataset-licensing rights, confidential data, privacy, AI Act GPAI/system roles and transitions, export/sanctions controls and service promises. A low parameter count or an open-source inference runtime is **not** a universal legal exception.

**Evidence to retain:** source/use permission ledger, dataset hashes and access rights, model releases, evaluation results, intended uses, known limitations, downstream licenses and restricted-market analysis.

## Case C: multiplayer game and paid content platform

A game uses third-party sprites, music tools, user-generated images, shared public profiles and auto-renewing premium subscriptions. Analyze commercial-use licensing of **each** asset (not merely the art editor's license); ROSCA and relevant state subscription rules; public content and abuse reporting; age-related privacy; and international sales controls. If public user media is hosted, assess actual statutory platform classification before invoking TAKE IT DOWN requirements.

## Graded exercise

Prepare a two-page applicability memo for one case, with 20 points each for factual completeness, statutory pinpoint/effective date, exceptions and alternative interpretations, engineering evidence, and explicit outstanding questions. A confident "compliant" answer without supporting facts should lose points.
