# Milestone 19A — Maryland MODPA: current sections and 2026 enacted amendments

**Research date:** November 13, 2026. **Scope:** targeted, original educational analysis of Maryland's current statutory text and HB 711 / Ch. 874. No attorney advice, compliance certification, exhaustive all-bills search or comprehensive case citator.

## 1. Citation versioning is part of legal reasoning

The original [2024 Chapter 454 / HB 567](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2024rs/chapters_noln/ch_454_hb0567e.pdf) enacted the Maryland Online Data Privacy Act (MODPA) in **Commercial Law §§14-4601–14-4614**. The **current Maryland General Assembly statutory text uses Commercial Law §§14-4701–14-4714**. The [2026 amendment (HB 711 / Ch. 874)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026rs/Chapters_noln/CH_874_hb0711t.pdf) itself cites the **2025 Replacement Volume** at §§14-4701, 14-4703, 14-4707 and 14-4712. Therefore **do not attribute the renumbering itself to HB 711**; distinguish the 2024 enacted citation, the later codification and 2026 modifications.

| Concept | Historical enactment | Current statutory citation |
|---|---|---|
| Definition, including sensitive data | §14-4601 | [§14-4701](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4701&enactments=false) |
| Coverage (35,000; or 10,000 plus more than 20% data-sales revenue) | §14-4602 | [§14-4702](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4702&enactments=false) |
| Entity and data exclusions | §14-4603 | [§14-4703](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4703&enactments=false) |
| Controller prohibitions and consent revocation | §14-4607 | [§14-4707](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4707&enactments=false) |
| Construction and exception provisions | §14-4612 | [§14-4712](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4712&enactments=false) |

**Temporal note:** The 2024 enactment's *general* effective date was **October 1, 2025**. Its uncodified Section 2 gave a particular §14-4612 prospectivity qualification for processing before **April 1, 2026** (now §14-4712). Do **not** silently convert this narrower history into an assertion that every duty had a universal April 2026 start. See the official 2024 chapter for the original context.

## 2. Enacted 2026 update: HB 711, Chapter 874

The Maryland General Assembly's [2026 HB0711 record](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/hb0711?ys=2026RS) identifies **Chapter 874**, enacted **May 31, 2026** under Maryland Constitution Article II §17(c), **effective July 1, 2026**. The [enacted 12-page chapter](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026rs/Chapters_noln/CH_874_hb0711t.pdf) was read and inspected as original official legislative text.

Important changes (actual enacted sections, rather than early introduced-bill drafts):

- **Inferred sensitive data:** [§14-4701(gg)(2)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4701&enactments=false) includes data inferred by a controller from personal data, alone or with other data, that indicates the sensitive categories in (gg)(1)(i)–(iv). An inferred health or location attribute is not automatically outside sensitive-data rules because it was **derived** rather than supplied.
- **Precise geolocation:** [§14-4701(x)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4701&enactments=false) refers to technology capable of identifying within **1,750 feet** the location of a **consumer, mobile device or vehicle**, with stated exclusions. Treat geolocation data categories and the exception text separately.
- **Public records and exemptions:** §14-4701(cc) now takes into account restrictions or terms of use imposed by a governmental entity on records used as publicly available information. [§14-4703(b)(8)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4703&enactments=false) has narrowed Driver's Privacy Protection Act-related exemption wording. Merely obtaining data from a public record does **not** erase all statutory questions.
- **Government-related data sales:** [§14-4707(a)(6)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4707&enactments=false) prohibits a controller **knowingly** selling consumer personal data to a federal, state or local governmental unit that, **within the preceding six months**, engaged in or supported civil immigration enforcement by personnel or material resources. This is **not** a universal ban on every public-record transaction or a rule that all government sales are prohibited.
- **Subpoena/cooperation construction:** [§14-4712(a)(2)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4712&enactments=false) limits reliance on specified construction exceptions involving governmental subpoenas and law-enforcement cooperation, with an express provision for **valid court warrants particularly describing the information**. It should not be taught as a blanket refusal requirement for all subpoenas or all law enforcement.

**Other amendments:** Ch. 874 also changes certain **General Provisions**, **Public Safety** and **State Government** provisions. They are **not** independently certified as an all-public-records treatise here.

## 3. Existing protections still matter

The [current §14-4707(a)(1)–(5)](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=gcl&section=14-4707&enactments=false) also addresses limits on sensitive collection/use, sale of sensitive data, discrimination, targeted advertising to known/constructively known under-18 consumers and sale of those consumers' personal data. §14-4707(b) requires data minimization/security protections and a consent-revocation mechanism with a **generally no-later-than-30-day stop-processing** rule. These pre-existing core protections should not be incorrectly presented as all created by HB 711.

## 4. Founder laboratory: two products and distinct actors

**Hypothetical A — candidate assessment platform.** A platform generates inferences from practice assessments, including user medical accommodations and inferred sensitive attributes. Determine **consumer vs employment-context** under §14-4701(h), data/controller roles, whether information reveals a statutory sensitive category, and whether the activity is a required consumer product use or an unrelated inference. Evaluate Title VII/ADA/FCRA independently; an exemption under one statute is not a general compliance certificate.

**Hypothetical B — games, analytics and data brokers.** A mobile game stores device-specific location coordinates and supplies a data partner with advertising audiences. Determine Maryland consumer counts and revenue, location precision, whether the sharing meets the statute's **sale** definition, age and sensitive inferences, and whether any governmental buyer or onward recipient meets §14-4707(a)(6)'s elements. An ordinary vendor contract or model-use label does not answer the statute.

**Evidence required:** dataset provenance; controller/processor contracts; device and vehicle location field mapping; inference provenance; users' data-rights and consent receipts; purpose/necessity assessment; vendor sale/recipient checks; present law and enacted amendments; legal uncertainties.
