# State AI law and automated employment decisions — 2026 update

**As of:** 2026-11-13. Distinguish a law's enactment, operative date, covered activity, legal actor and exceptions. Examples below are original hypotheticals.

## Colorado: SB 26-189 replaces the original SB 24-205 architecture

[SB 26-189, Colorado General Assembly](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189) became law on May 14, 2026 (2026 Session Laws ch. 131). It **repeals and reenacts** the earlier statutory framework for automated decision-making technology (ADMT). Do not teach the 2024 high-risk AI requirements as the unqualified current 2027 duties. The [Colorado Attorney General's ADMT rulemaking page](https://coag.gov/ai/) identifies January 1, 2027 as the main operative date.

The enacted law addresses ADMT that materially influences consequential decisions, including employment, education, housing, finance, insurance, health care and essential government services. The legislative summary identifies developer technical documentation, material-change information, retention, consumer notice, access/correction and meaningful human review/reconsideration provisions, subject to statutory definitions and exceptions. It describes public enforcement through the attorney general and no new private action under this enactment. **Timing caveat:** portions related to agency rulemaking and specified statutory subsections took effect on passage, while most substantive requirements begin January 2027. Confirm the signed act and the Colorado code rather than treating the bill summary as itself the law.

**Exercise:** A startup supplies a ranking API used by a Colorado employer. Separate provider/deployer roles, intended use, whether a score materially influences the final employment decision, protected data, documentation delivered to the employer, and actual date of use. Do not conclude that all generic embeddings or simple spell checkers are covered.

## Illinois: employment AI rules effective January 1, 2026

[775 ILCS 5/2-102(L)](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ILCS/details?ActID=2266&ActName=Illinois+Human+Rights+Act.&ChapAct=FullText&Chapter=&ChapterID=64&MajorTopic=&SeqStart=) addresses certain employer uses of AI in recruitment, hiring, promotion and related employment decisions, including discriminatory effects or zip-code proxies and a notice obligation. Public Act 103-804 introduced the relevant 2026 effective date. **Critical version warning:** the General Assembly page contains versions before and after subsequent amendments; choose the version effective on the facts' date, not merely the last text displayed. Departmental implementation details require a current rules check.

**Exercise:** A recruiter uses a vendor's automated candidate score. Record the employer's use, notice content and delivery, job-relatedness and adverse-impact testing. A vendor accuracy benchmark does not independently prove a lawful hiring practice.

## New York City: Local Law 144, not every automation

[NYC DCWP's AEDT guidance](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page) explains covered automated employment decision tools (AEDTs) and the employer/employment-agency conditions: a bias audit within one year, public audit information and required notices. [Official FAQs](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/businesses/faq-automated-employment-decision-tools.page) describe substantial assistance/replacement criteria, the independent-audit concept and specific notices. Enforcement began July 5, 2023. The legal classification turns on the tool's function and employer's use; do not assume an AI text editor is an AEDT solely because it uses a model.

## California: distinguish rule effective date from feature deadlines

The [California Privacy Protection Agency's final ADMT/risk/audit regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates) were approved September 22, 2025 and became **effective January 1, 2026**. The Agency says certain implementation obligations phase in during 2027–2028. Check **title 11 CCR**, regulated-business thresholds, applicable ADMT definitions, exemptions, actual use and deadlines before declaring a feature subject to access or opt-out controls. Do not describe every agency compliance milestone as starting January 1, 2026.
