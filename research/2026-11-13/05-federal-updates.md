# Federal consumer, employment and platform law — 2026 source clinic

**As of:** 2026-11-13. Statute, regulation, withdrawn agency guidance and a court order are different authorities.

## SaaS recurring charges

[15 U.S.C. § 8403](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t15/s8403/) (ROSCA) governs certain online negative-option transactions. Its requirements include disclosing material terms before collecting billing data, express informed consent before charge and a simple recurring-charge stopping mechanism. These statutory obligations did **not disappear** with the judicial vacatur of the FTC's 2024 amended Negative Option Rule.

The [FTC's February 2026 correction](https://www.ftc.gov/policy/public-comments/revision-negative-option-rule-withdrawal-cars-rule-removal-non-compete-rule-conform-these-rules-0) restored the pre-2024 rule text. Teaching the vacated 2024 click-to-cancel amendment as current nationwide binding text is wrong; stating all cancellation requirements vanished is equally wrong. State automatic-renewal laws and FTC Act §5 require separate analysis.

## Algorithmic employment reports

The [CFPB's withdrawn-guidance index](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/guidance/withdrawn-guidance/) records **Circular 2024-06** on employment background dossiers and algorithmic scores as **withdrawn May 12, 2025**. This withdrawal did **not repeal** the Fair Credit Reporting Act. Vendor/consumer-reporting-agency status, consumer-report nature, permissible purpose and adverse-action duties must be decided from statutory elements and actual business activity, not a product label.

The [EEOC's employment-test guidance](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/employment-tests-and-selection-procedures) is not itself binding law. Title VII, the ADA and context-specific job-related selection validation remain distinct. A 95% generic benchmark is not enough to establish job validity.

## TAKE IT DOWN Act

[Public Law 119-12](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-119publ12), enacted May 19, 2025, has platform duties concerning certain nonconsensual intimate visual depictions and a 2026 implementation phase for covered-platform removal processes. Whether a service is a covered platform, whether a valid notice was submitted and the affected content's nature matter. Do not treat every private inference API or locally run editor as automatically covered merely because it generates images.

## Questions for students

1. A seven-day free trial converts to paid service with renewal terms hidden in footer text. Analyze ROSCA and state law separately from the vacated FTC 2024 rule.
2. A hiring-score service markets a "verified candidate report." Identify facts needed for FCRA classification and distinguish current statute from withdrawn CFPB circular.
3. Compare a private AI photo editor with a public user-upload gallery under the TAKE IT DOWN Act's covered-platform definition.
