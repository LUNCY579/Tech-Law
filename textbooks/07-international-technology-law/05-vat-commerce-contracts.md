# Chapter 5 — Cross-border SaaS pricing, VAT, contracts and consumer rights

## 1. Taxes follow transactions, not just servers
A U.S. corporation selling subscriptions to EU consumers must investigate **place-of-supply**, B2C/B2B classification, VAT registration/collection, invoicing, exemptions, intermediary or marketplace liability and possible local nexus. The existence of a Stripe or app-store integration does not itself prove all taxes are handled.

The EU One Stop Shop (OSS) schemes may simplify reporting for eligible cross-border supplies but do not erase underlying VAT liability. The EU's **VAT in the Digital Age** reforms adopted March 2025 phase in through 2035; the Commission identifies OSS changes starting **1 January 2027**, single-VAT-registration measures in 2028 and broader digital reporting in 2030, subject to specifics. [Official European Commission ViDA timeline](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-digital-age-vida_en).

## 2. Customer classification matters
B2B VAT handling may differ materially from B2C electronic services. Collect only necessary evidence to determine customer jurisdiction and valid business status, while complying with privacy obligations. A customer typing “business” into a text field is not conclusive proof of relevant tax treatment.

## 3. Contract stack for international SaaS
A cross-border SaaS contract often includes MSA / order form / DPA / SCC transfer instrument / SLA / terms of acceptable use / subscription and cancellation terms. Compare:
- party identities and contract precedence;
- governing law versus nonwaivable local mandatory rules;
- dispute forum, arbitration, service of process;
- processor and subprocessor roles;
- international transfer obligations, security and incident notice;
- taxes, currency, payment providers, refunds, renewals;
- IP licenses and local restrictions;
- export/sanctions compliance and termination.

An American governing-law clause does not automatically waive mandatory EU consumer or privacy protections.

## 4. Consumer-facing product example
A U.S. document SaaS offers a seven-day trial at €12/month to EU consumers. You need a separately scoped review of B2C price disclosure, withdrawal/cancellation rules, subscription terms, VAT and receipts. U.S. ROSCA and state renewal rules may also matter for domestic customers, but are not a replacement for EU consumer requirements.

## 5. Marketplace versus direct merchant
If an app marketplace processes payments, determine contractually and legally whether the platform, startup or both act as seller/supplier and which taxes/consumer obligations each assumes. **Never assume** third-party processor service terms transfer all liability automatically.

## Exam
A SaaS founder insists that because the company is incorporated in Delaware and invoices through Stripe, “we do not owe EU VAT.” Provide five questions: Who receives the supply? What is delivered? Where is the customer legally located? Who is the supplier/merchant of record? Which VAT regime and registration thresholds apply?

**Model answer:** Neither incorporation nor payment processor dictates all tax obligations. A qualified cross-border tax review should determine the correct applicable route before checkout goes live.
