# Chapter 6 — International AI startup commercialization capstone

**Total:** 100 points. Scenario invented. Use exact source sections and distinguish applicability from readiness.

## Case: Atlas Passport AI
A Delaware AI company sells candidate scoring and verified credentials to German employers and California employers. It hosts its database in Virginia, routes some prompts to a U.S. model vendor with thirty-day logs, grants access to overseas support contractors, lets users publish credential pages, and offers subscriptions to EU individual users. Its website says “GDPR certified,” “all data stays in Europe,” “all global markets supported” and “job performance scientifically proven.” It intends to distribute downloadable model weights globally.

### Questions

**1. Jurisdiction (15):** Identify at least six legally significant geographic/actor facts, and explain why a Delaware choice-of-law clause is not decisive.  
**2. Transfers (20):** Construct a GDPR Chapter V map with DPF/SCC/adequacy, role determination and onward transfers.  
**3. EU regulation (20):** Analyze AI Act scope/Annex III employment rules, effective dates, GDPR automated decision rules and potential DSA effects of public credentials.  
**4. Export/sanctions (20):** Provide classification and screening decision tree for downloads and overseas support access.  
**5. Taxes/contract (15):** Distinguish B2C/B2B VAT, payment intermediary roles and cross-border contract precedence.  
**6. Marketing claims (10):** Replace four unsupported claims with appropriately bounded and verifiable statements.

---

## Model answer key

### 1 (15)
U.S. incorporation, EU employer establishment, EU candidate locations, EU individual buyers, database/model/vendor location, remote contractor location, output use, sales targeting and payment status can independently matter. GDPR Art. 3 and AI Act Art. 2 should be examined. Contract governing law does not erase mandatory legal obligations.

### 2 (20)
First determine controller/processor, lawful basis, GDPR territorial reach and whether a Chapter V transfer occurs. Map EU→U.S. SaaS and vendor/onward flows and support access. Under Art. 45 the DPF adequacy decision applies to eligible participating U.S. organizations (not all U.S. recipients), while Art. 46 SCCs require actual valid modules and transfer assessment. “GDPR certified” cannot be inferred from DPF eligibility.

### 3 (20)
Analyze employment-system provider/deployer roles, Art. 6/Annex III conditions, phased high-risk dates (official 2026 version anticipates Annex III requirements December 2, 2027), plus GDPR Art. 22 automated decisions where material. Public credential publishing could implicate DSA only if the service satisfies relevant intermediary/platform definitions; do not assume every shared profile makes the entire firm a very large platform.

### 4 (20)
EAR Part 734 subject status; classify each artifact (not simply model family name), destination/end-user/end-use; Part 736 prohibitions, Part 740 exceptions, Part 742 controls; examine publication and encryption rules. Screen OFAC programs separately and assess remote releases. A blanket “available everywhere” promise is not responsible.

### 5 (15)
Tax analysis must identify supplier, purchaser, item/service, customer VAT status, place-of-supply, intermediary arrangements and the right EU VAT mechanism. Cross-border SaaS order forms and DPA/SCC may contain different precedence/roles. U.S. incorporation and Stripe use do not eliminate tax review.

### 6 (10)
Examples conditional on evidence: “We publish the exact regions and third-party subprocessors used”; “Our credential signature verifies issuer-authored data, not predictive scientific validity”; “International availability depends on applicable export, sanctions, privacy and market rules”; “We have implemented specified privacy controls, and our transfer mechanisms are documented where applicable.” Do not claim any of these until supported by real implementation.

## Scoring
Legal accuracy (30), role/scope (20), regulatory timing (15), evidence and technical controls (20), candid uncertainty (15). Passing >=85 indicates course understanding only; it is neither a legal licence nor compliance certificate.
