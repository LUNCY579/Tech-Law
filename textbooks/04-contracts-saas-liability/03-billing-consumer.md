# Chapter 3 — Subscriptions, cancellations and consumer-protection law

## 1. The 2026 correction many startup checklists miss
The FTC's widely publicized **2024 amended click-to-cancel Negative Option Rule** was **vacated by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025**. In **February 2026**, the FTC formally recodified the pre-2024 rule text. Therefore it is **incorrect** to teach the vacated 2024 amendment as a currently operative nationwide rule. See [FTC February 11, 2026 action](https://www.ftc.gov/policy/public-comments/revision-negative-option-rule-withdrawal-cars-rule-removal-non-compete-rule-conform-these-rules-0) and [FTC federal litigation report](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2025-12-Final-Public.pdf). State automatic-renewal laws and other federal laws may independently apply.

## 2. ROSCA still matters
Under [15 U.S.C. §8403](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t15/s8403/), covered online negative-option transactions require (1) clear and conspicuous disclosure of all material terms **before** obtaining billing information; (2) consumer's express informed consent before charging; (3) simple mechanism to stop recurring charges. This is **an enacted federal law**, separate from the vacated FTC amendment. Also examine [FTC Act §5](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/45) against deceptive checkout or cancellation claims.

## 3. Implementation analysis
**Before payment:** show recurring price, payment frequency, trial end, material restrictions, cancellation and user assent.  
**When billing:** maintain correct consent evidence, pricing/version, invoices and renewal changes.  
**When cancelling:** provide simple stopping mechanisms as required by the actual applicable rule; cancel billing state across payment processors and schedules and confirm effect.  
**After cancellation:** preserve narrow necessary receipts/records; honor deletion rights where applicable.

State-specific law (for example, California automatic-renewal provisions) can impose additional requirements. Don't assume federal law alone resolves the question.

## 4. Worked test
A trial says “Free for 7 days,” automatically bills $29/mo afterwards, hides renewal conditions on a separate footer page, and requires contacting a nonresponsive phone line to cancel. Potential ROSCA/FTC/state-law problems depend on full facts. A term buried where the user never sees it and impractical cancellation are substantial red flags.

## 5. Separate marketing emails
[CAN-SPAM Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/7704) regulates commercial messages; certain transactional/relationship messages are treated differently. Email classification depends on content and primary purpose, not merely whether the sender calls it “account service.” Do not require a marketing opt-out in every password-reset message as a blanket statement.

### Exam
**Prompt:** The founder cites a 2024 FTC news release as proof that its 2024 click-to-cancel requirements are currently binding.  
**Model answer:** Incorrect as of November 2026. The 2025 judicial vacatur and 2026 FTC correction must be considered. Analyze operative pre-2024 regulation, ROSCA §8403, FTC Act §5, and relevant state automatic-renewal laws independently.
