# Chapter 1 — Contract formation and digital consent

## 1. Identify the agreement
The usual analytical categories are offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity and legality, plus any required statutory formalities and applicable state law. Online terms introduce an evidentiary problem: whether a reasonable user received legally adequate notice and manifested assent. An emailed document or a footer link alone does not prove assent.

### Interface evidence
For each signup, payment or AI-service escalation retain (appropriately): exact displayed terms/version/hash, UI variant, timestamp, user action, locale, link placement, and accessible copy. These records support proof but **cannot repair** deficient notice by themselves.

## 2. Arbitration requires agreement
[Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C. §2](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/9/2) makes qualifying arbitration agreements enforceable subject to recognized legal grounds for revocation; do not infer an arbitration clause governs an individual merely because it is posted online.

### Case — *Coinbase, Inc. v. Suski*, 602 U.S. 143 (2024)
[Official Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/23-3_879d.pdf). Users had an account agreement with delegation/arbitration and separate sweepstakes rules pointing to court. **Issue:** who decides which agreement governs the arbitrability conflict? **Holding:** court decides that antecedent contract conflict. **Reasoning:** arbitration depends on what parties agreed; an earlier delegation does not predetermine whether it or the competing contract controls. **Limit:** no universal rule that later agreements always win.

### Historical case — *Specht v. Netscape*, 306 F.3d 17 (2d Cir. 2002)
Often cited for inadequate notice in a software download interface; **official original opinion authentication remains pending in this repository**. Do not cite fabricated official-host URLs or passage pinpoints. Use as qualified background rather than authenticated case study until source work is finished.

## 3. Multi-document SaaS scenario
A workspace user accepts standard Terms with arbitration, then later signs an enterprise pilot order that gives Missouri courts exclusive jurisdiction over pilot disputes. Identify the **parties, subject matter, version dates, integration and precedence provisions, assent proof**. Under *Coinbase*, courts may need to decide which agreement governs a threshold conflict, before merits/compulsion can be resolved.

## Exercises
1. Draw a wireframe where the user clearly and affirmatively accepts v2 Terms; explain how to preserve evidence without logging passwords.
2. Write a 150-word memo distinguishing a “browsewrap” footer and affirmative clickwrap consent under applicable contract law.
3. A customer disputes a term changed only in a silent website update. Identify formation, notice, unilateral modification, material-change and consumer-law questions.

**Model conclusion:** Do not announce enforceability from UI labels alone. Identify relevant jurisdiction, notice and affirmative user conduct, exact documents and precedence.
