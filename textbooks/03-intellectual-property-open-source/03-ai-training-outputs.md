# Chapter 3 — Training data, generated outputs and AI copyright

## 1. Four different legal acts
Data **acquisition**, data **copying/processing**, model **distribution**, and generated-output **publication** may involve different rights. A model license allowing commercial inference does not necessarily authorize downloading a prohibited training corpus or redistributing model weights. Analyze contractual access controls and privacy separately.

## 2. U.S. Copyright Office: official report, not a new statute
In its [January 29, 2025 Part 2 report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/), the U.S. Copyright Office explained its approach to copyrightability of AI outputs: human-selected creative expression and arrangements may qualify, while prompts alone generally do not confer authorship of all generated expression. The report is **official agency analysis**, not a Supreme Court holding or independent legislation.

**Example:** A human composes a scene, draws original character sprites and uses an AI tool to produce textures. Rights analysis is work-by-work and authorship-specific; do not claim every generated pixel is automatically protected or automatically public domain. The tool's service agreement and third-party infringement issues remain distinct.

## 3. Training and fair-use questions
For copyrighted training works ask: how were data acquired, what copies created, what purpose, amount, market alternatives, outputs and substitutions? Apply [§107](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/17/107) to the **actual alleged use**, with case history reviewed as of transaction date. *Google* concerns Java APIs; *Warhol* concerns particular commercial licensing of art; neither universally resolves LLM-training litigation. Avoid claiming that any contested training practice has a settled blanket legal outcome.

## 4. Model weights and memorization
Weights, tokenizer files, prompts, adapter parameters, and retrieval indexes can all have differing legal characteristics. Whether copyright subsists in particular weights or a given output is an unsettled or fact-specific question. Explicit weight-license terms, training data provenance, reproducible evaluation and output copyright screening should be tracked independently.

**Hypothetical:** A startup fine-tunes a commercially licensed model on a scraped magazine archive, then generates a near-verbatim magazine article. Distinguish (i) underlying archive access/copyright; (ii) model license; (iii) training/fair use; (iv) possible output infringement; (v) terms and transparency promises; (vi) dataset privacy.

## 5. Governance controls
Maintain a provenance manifest; dataset-specific opt-outs and removal policy as applicable; artifact hashes; license policy at exact revisions; near-duplicate output testing; request and takedown intake; data retention timeline; legal hold; and a claims registry. These are engineering practices, not a claim that each is universally mandatory.

### Exam (25 points)
Explain why “generated by AI” does not alone answer whether a commercial asset is (a) copyrightable, (b) licensed to your company, or (c) infringing. Cite §102, §106, §107 and the Copyright Office report. **Answer:** the three questions concern different legal relationships, and need separate facts and authority.
