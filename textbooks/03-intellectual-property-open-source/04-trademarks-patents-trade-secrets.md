# Chapter 4 — Brand, patent and trade-secret strategy

## 1. Trademark identifies source, not ownership of an idea
The USPTO emphasizes **likelihood of confusion**: similarity in sound, appearance or commercial impression combined with related goods/services. An available domain name, GitHub organization or business registration does not guarantee clearance. [USPTO official guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion). Also read [15 U.S.C. §1114](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/1114) and [§1125](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/1125) for relevant federal trademark causes of action, subject to statutory elements.

**Example:** Choosing “Rineya Cloud” when an existing similarly named software mark exists calls for product category, channels, market evidence and search, not just exact-match text search.

## 2. Patents protect certain inventions under specified conditions
Review [35 U.S.C. §101](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/35/101) (subject matter), [§102](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/35/102) (novelty), [§103](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/35/103) (nonobviousness), [§112](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/35/112) (description and claims). Patent eligibility for software/abstract ideas is a specialized field. **A repository README is not a patentability opinion.** Public disclosures can impact rights, including overseas.

## 3. Trade secrets depend on secrecy measures
Under [18 U.S.C. §1839(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/18/1839), the federal trade secret definition includes qualifying information where the owner took reasonable secrecy measures and the information derives independent economic value from not being generally known/readily ascertainable. Mere “confidential” markings without meaningful controls may be inadequate.

**Example:** An internal model tuning recipe with restricted access, employee agreements, audit logs and limited sharing may qualify depending on facts; a fully public training recipe generally faces a different secrecy analysis.

## 4. Contrasting the IP rights
| Subject | Potential right | First step |
|---|---|---|
| Source code | Copyright / trade secret / contract | Ownership and licensing |
| Novel technical implementation | Patent (if eligible) | Novelty/eligibility counsel |
| Product name | Trademark | Clearance search |
| Secret launch dataset | Contract/privacy/trade secret | Lawful acquisition and secrecy |
| Game character art | Copyright / possible trademark | Provenance, expressive copying |

### Case exercise
A former contractor shares private code and publishes an allegedly confusingly similar product name. Analyze assignment, copyright, potential trade-secret misappropriation, contract confidentiality and trademark separately. **Answer:** multiple potentially overlapping rights can apply; establish elements, ownership and evidence for each, without presuming infringement.
