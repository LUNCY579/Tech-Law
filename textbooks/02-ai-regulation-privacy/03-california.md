# Chapter 3 — California privacy law and 2026–2027 obligations

## 3.1 First decide whether the CCPA applies
The California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended, applies to covered businesses meeting statutory criteria and subject to various exceptions. State of incorporation alone is not the decisive question. Determine consumer and personal-information definitions, business thresholds, affiliates, exemptions and actual California-directed conduct. Read the statutory text and official implementing rules separately.

**Official materials:** [CPPA laws and regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/); [California Civil Code §§1798.100 et seq.](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=3.&part=4.&lawCode=CIV&title=1.81.5).

## 3.2 Consumer rights and truthful operational design
Data inventory → notice → verify rights requests → respond according to legal deadlines/exceptions → propagate actions through systems → retain decision evidence. Distinguish sale/share restrictions from erasure; distinguish service provider/contractor roles and permissible uses. A privacy notice cannot grant permission for anything otherwise prohibited by law.

**Illustration:** Candidate requests deletion while records are on litigation hold. A correct workflow identifies which portions can be deleted now, which may be retained under a recognized exception, and what is told to the consumer. Neither “erase absolutely everything instantly” nor “retain forever” follows from the initial request.

## 3.3 What changed for 2026?
The CPPA adopted regulations on **September 22, 2025**, with general effective date **January 1, 2026**. The package addresses updated CCPA rules, certain cybersecurity audits, risk assessments and automated decisionmaking technology (ADMT). **Different compliance milestones apply**; don't say every ADMT requirement became due on January 1, 2026.

The CPPA's official [September 23, 2025 announcement](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html) states:
- Risk-assessment requirements for covered activities begin January 1, 2026, with later submission requirements.
- Covered ADMT used for significant decisions must comply beginning **January 1, 2027**.
- Some cybersecurity audit certification deadlines are phased by revenue into **2028–2030**.

These are agency summaries; read actual [approved regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates) before assigning any obligation to a specific company.

## 3.4 ADMT classification for hiring
A candidate scoring/recruiting product should determine whether the specific covered business uses automated decisionmaking technology to make a “significant decision” as defined by regulations, including the operative exceptions and timing. A general purpose assistant assisting with drafting is not necessarily regulated identically to an automated hiring evaluation.

**Evidence worksheet:** Decision description; consequences; human reviewer involvement; use of personal information; model and version; threshold; available opt-out/access rights where required; notice; data retention; compliance start date; evaluation of exemptions.

## 3.5 Risk assessments and cybersecurity audits
Avoid two mistakes: assuming every small startup must perform every audit, and assuming every small startup is exempt. Apply the regulations' triggers, scope, schedule and any sector-specific constraints. A good engineering risk register is prudent even when statutory assessment reporting is not mandatory.

### Exercise
A hiring SaaS processes California applicant records in 2026 but does not reach the relevant business threshold. Does every 2026 regulation automatically bind it?  
**Answer:** No. First resolve covered-business status, role, data type, exemptions and effective provisions. A vendor contract or other law might nevertheless require safeguards.
