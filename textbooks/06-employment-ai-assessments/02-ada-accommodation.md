# Chapter 2 — ADA: disability, accommodation and algorithmic screening

## Statute first
[42 U.S.C. §12112(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t42/s12112/) prohibits discrimination against qualified individuals on the basis of disability in covered employment contexts. Subsection **(b)(5)** addresses reasonable accommodation and undue hardship; subsection **(b)(6)** addresses qualification standards, employment tests and selection criteria screening out individuals with disabilities, subject to statutory conditions. **§12112(d)** restricts disability-related inquiries and medical examinations at different employment stages.

## EEOC guidance
The [EEOC's AI and ADA collection](https://www.eeoc.gov/eeoc-disability-related-resources/artificial-intelligence-and-ada) links its 2022 technical assistance. Agency examples include speech-pattern scoring that disadvantages someone who stutters and inaccessible test interfaces. [EEOC visual-disability guidance](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/visual-disabilities-workplace-and-americans-disabilities-act) addresses accommodations in algorithmic assessment. Guidance is informative but not itself a substitute for the binding statute and regulations.

## Worked scenarios
1. An automated interview rates voice fluency, but the job is primarily written programming. Ask whether speech fluency is essential to the actual role and whether a validated alternative format is available.
2. A coding test has a countdown timer and no keyboard-only workflow. Some candidates may be screened out because of disability; evaluate accommodation, essential job functions, undue hardship and accessible alternatives.
3. Before a conditional job offer, a chatbot asks an applicant to disclose a diagnosis. Review **§12112(d)** and precise exceptions; do not assume general AI consent authorizes prohibited medical inquiries.

## Product controls
Accessible test flow, a clear accommodation contact and process, alternate format availability, no unnecessary medical questions, reviewer access limited by role, confidentiality of disability information, and a documented accommodation decision. Do not use protected health details to construct hidden “fit” scores.

## Exercise / model answer
**Prompt:** A blind applicant fails an image-only assessment. The employer says “everybody took the same test, therefore it is fair.”  
**Answer:** Equal presentation does not resolve whether a qualified person needed reasonable accommodation, whether the screening criteria are job-related, or whether a less exclusionary alternative was required. Apply §12112(b)(5), (b)(6) and relevant EEOC guidance to actual job functions and facts.
