# Chapter 1 — Title VII: automated hiring as an employment practice

## 1. Binding statutory elements
[42 U.S.C. §2000e-2(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t42/s2000e-2/) prohibits specified employer discrimination because of race, color, religion, sex or national origin. Section **2000e-2(k)(1)(A)** describes disparate-impact proof, job-relatedness/business necessity and alternative employment practices; read the full subsection, including challenged practice specificity and provisions for analyzing decision processes that cannot be separated.

## 2. Disparate treatment versus disparate impact
- **Treatment:** Was a protected characteristic used in an intentionally discriminatory employment decision? Evaluate evidence and applicable legal proof.
- **Impact:** Was a specific facially neutral practice shown to produce prohibited group impact, and what job-related/business-necessity defense or alternative-practice issues arise?
A performance gap or subgroup disparity alone cannot conclusively establish all statutory elements.

## 3. Official EEOC discussion
[EEOC Employment Tests and Selection Procedures](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/employment-tests-and-selection-procedures) explains selection practices and validation issues, and links the historical [*Griggs v. Duke Power Co.*, 401 U.S. 424 (1971)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/401bv.pdf) doctrine. The bound-volume link is an official volume, **not a verified page-specific image in this textbook**; do not invent exact pinpoint quotations from it.

**Case reasoning lead:** *Griggs* illustrates the importance of employment selection criteria and job-relatedness; its later statutory treatment in 1991 and current §2000e-2(k) must be read together. Not every difference in test outcomes establishes liability.

## 4. AI hiring product example
An AI platform assigns a suitability score to applicant coding exercises. An employer filters everyone below 60. Needed evidence: precise position and competencies, actual test items, intended use and cut-off rationale, measurement quality, applicant population, group selection rates, accommodations, alternatives and human decision control. A signature verifying the score does not demonstrate that a 60 cutoff lawfully predicts job success.

### Exercises
**Q:** An automated résumé parser never receives race in input features. Does that guarantee no disparate impact?  
**Answer:** No. Protected-class proxies, differential data patterns, selection procedures and actual outcomes require analysis; lack of explicit race input is not a complete legal defense.

**Q:** A vendor shows 95% benchmark accuracy. Is this job-related validity evidence?  
**Answer:** Only if the benchmark, labels, construct, population and use correspond to the actual job and legal standards. Generic classification accuracy alone is insufficient.
