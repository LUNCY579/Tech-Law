# Chapter 2 — Founder shares, restricted stock, vesting and intellectual property

## 2.1 Shares have multiple counts
**Authorized** shares: ceiling under charter (subject to classes, amendments and other constraints). **Issued**: shares actually issued; **outstanding**: issued shares held outside treasury; **reserved**: shares set aside for future potential equity issuances; **fully diluted**: an analytical denominator based on defined assumptions about options, warrants, convertibles and pools. Fully diluted is not a single statutory formula.

**Worked example (illustrative):** 12,000,000 authorized; 8,000,000 outstanding founder shares; 2,000,000 options reserved but unissued; 2,000,000 unallocated. Founders hold 100% of **outstanding** shares, but 80% of the illustrative 10M **fully diluted** denominator. The reserved options are not automatically current outstanding stock.

## 2.2 Vesting is a contractual structure
A common illustration is four-year vesting with one-year cliff, but the actual vesting conditions, repurchase rights, termination triggers and acceleration are negotiated. A founder may initially **own** restricted stock that the company can repurchase if vesting conditions fail; other arrangements use future grants. These produce different legal/tax outcomes.

**Build a founder stock checklist:** recipient name; number/class; purchase price and paid consideration; board authorization; signatures; vesting/repurchase schedule; securities exemption; ledger entry; potential tax filings; assignment of inventions; relevant disclosure of prior inventions.

## 2.3 Section 83 and the 83(b) election
**26 U.S.C. §83** generally addresses property transferred in connection with services and the timing of income inclusion; an election under §83(b), where eligible, can change the timing. The [IRS Form 15620 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f15620.pdf) state that an 83(b) election generally must be filed **within 30 days after the property is transferred**, with specified weekend/holiday rules. This is a serious deadline; the election is not automatically advisable and may have adverse tax consequences if value declines or stock is forfeited. Consult tax counsel before issuance and election.

**Important:** An 83(b) election does not substitute for corporate issuance, a signed stock agreement, an IP assignment or a securities exemption.

## 2.4 IP ownership matters before fundraising
If founders write the company's core model, source code or game assets before incorporation, investigate the **chain of title**. Who created the work? During employment or using another company's resources? What inventions agreement applies? What is validly assigned? Third-party open-source licenses, trademarks, data rights and patents require separate treatment. A unilateral statement “the startup owns everything I made” is not by itself a complete legal audit.

**Founder hypothetical:** Alice trains a model on a university workstation, then later incorporates a startup to sell it. **Questions:** university IP and computing-resource policies? employment/contract obligations? dataset provenance? third-party model license? contributor rights? assignability? These must be researched before investor representations.

## 2.5 Tax and securities risk register
| Event | Possible legal issue | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Founder stock issuance | authorization and securities compliance | board consent, stock purchase agreement, ledger |
| Restricted stock | §83 timing and elections | transfer date and tax advice |
| Option plan | grant authority, securities/tax issues | plan, board approval, grant agreement |
| Contractor authors code | IP ownership, employment status | agreement and provenance |
| Co-founder departs | vesting and repurchase rights | executed documents and dates |

### Examination
**Question:** Is it safe to wait 60 days after receiving restricted stock before considering a §83(b) election?  
**Model answer:** No. Where an election is appropriate, the statutory timing rule is generally 30 days after property transfer, subject to limited timing rules. Waiting can forfeit the opportunity. Verify transfer date and eligibility immediately with counsel.
