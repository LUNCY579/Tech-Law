# Chapter 4 — Securities exemptions, investor solicitation and compliance

## 4.1 Start with the registration-or-exemption rule
**Securities Act §5, 15 U.S.C. §77e** generally prohibits securities offers and sales without registration absent an applicable exemption. An exempt offer is still subject to anti-fraud rules and possibly state notice fees and other obligations. A SAFE can be a security; “friends and family” does not mean automatically exempt.

**Source:** [Official U.S. Code](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/15/77e); [SEC exempt offerings](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings).

## 4.2 Compare routes before contacting investors

| Route | General solicitation | Purchaser considerations | Key caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section 4(a)(2) | Generally incompatible with public offering | Private-offering facts and access to information | Fact-intensive; not a universal safe harbor |
| Regulation D Rule 506(b) | **Prohibited** for the offering | Accredited investors unrestricted in count; limited sophisticated non-accredited purchasers subject to conditions | Reg D, disclosure and Form D requirements |
| Rule 506(c) | **Permitted** | All purchasers accredited; issuer reasonably verifies status | Self-certification alone may be inadequate |
| Regulation Crowdfunding | Public campaign through qualifying intermediary under rules | Specific funding/issuer/investor limits | Do not self-host a public equity sale outside the framework |
| Rule 701 | Compensatory, not capital raising | Eligible recipients and disclosure conditions | Not for general investor fundraising |

**2026 verification:** The SEC's [Rule 506(c) page](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/general-solicitation-rule-506c), updated March 17, 2026, confirms accredited-purchaser verification and Form D timing; [Rule 506(b) guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/private-placements-rule-506b) explains the no-general-solicitation boundary. Always check newly amended rules and whether they are effective on the transaction date.

## 4.3 What constitutes marketing a security?
The SEC treats unrestricted websites, mass advertisements and other communications arousing public investor interest as possible general solicitation. The precise facts matter. There is a difference between describing a product publicly and actively pitching security sales. A “we are raising, message me for guaranteed 3x returns” post creates overlapping solicitation and anti-fraud concerns.

**Source:** [SEC General Solicitation guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/general-solicitation).

## 4.4 Form D and states
The SEC explains that Reg D issuers generally file Form D **within 15 days after first sale** for the covered offerings; state regulators may still require notices and fees despite federal preemption of some state registration rules. Filing Form D does not itself make an otherwise unlawful transaction lawful.  
**Source:** [SEC Exempt Offerings FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/frequently-asked-questions-about-exempt-offerings).

## 4.5 Anti-fraud even when exempt
Carefully document factual claims about the startup, cap table, IP ownership, customer revenue, benchmark performance, contracts and material risks. A pitch deck's “our 0.6B model beats every leading model” claim demands evaluation context and evidence. Never advertise guaranteed returns or knowingly omit critical risks.

## 4.6 Realistic fundraise gate
**Before first offer:** identify issuer and security; select available exemption with counsel; define intended recipients and outreach; prepare accurate disclosure and capitalization documents; verify recipient eligibility and bad-actor conditions; document approvals; schedule filings; document related state-law requirements.

**Case study (hypothetical):** A founder places a public equity-offer form on a game landing page while planning Rule 506(b). Ask whether public solicitation has already occurred; seek securities counsel before further outreach. Do not solve it by merely deleting the page and assuming the legal problem vanished.

### Examination
**Q:** Can you publicly advertise a 506(c) offering and rely only on a buyer's unchecked “I am accredited” box?  
**Answer:** Rule 506(c) permits general solicitation under conditions, but requires reasonable steps to verify accredited-investor status and all purchasers must be accredited; a checkbox alone is not a universally sufficient verification procedure.

**Sources:** [SEC Rule 506(c)](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/general-solicitation-rule-506c), [SEC Rule 506(b)](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/private-placements-rule-506b).
