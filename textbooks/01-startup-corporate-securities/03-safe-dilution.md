# Chapter 3 — SAFEs, capitalization and dilution

## 3.1 What a SAFE represents
A Simple Agreement for Future Equity is a **contractual financing instrument**, not instantly the same as a fully issued common share. Conversion depends on the specific document, valuation cap, discount provisions, equity-financing definitions, liquidity/dissolution provisions and capitalization definitions. “SAFE” is not an exemption from securities law. This course intentionally does **not** reproduce proprietary financing templates.

## 3.2 Pre-money and post-money
Define the denominator before calculating ownership. A $1M instrument with a “$10M cap” does **not** guarantee 10% current ownership in every structure. A simplified post-money capped SAFE heuristic (investment / post-money cap) may estimate the SAFE's ownership immediately before a priced round **under the particular instrument's assumptions**; multiple SAFEs, option pools, discounts, outstanding convertibles and definitions change real outcomes.

## 3.3 Worked scenario A — priced share issuance
Assume 8M founder shares and 2M reserved options (10M fully diluted before issuance). Investor buys **2M newly issued shares**. Post-issuance fully diluted denominator = 12M: founder 8/12 = **66.67%**; pool 2/12 = **16.67%**; investor 2/12 = **16.67%**. This is a direct share-issuance example, **not SAFE conversion**.

## 3.4 Worked scenario B — simplified cap intuition
Company raises $500k on an illustrative $5M post-money cap and later $1M on a separate $10M post-money cap. Naively adding 10% + 10% is a **teaching approximation**, not legally sufficient to compute final ownership. Ask: are there multiple post-money SAFEs? What counts as Company Capitalization? How do later SAFE sales affect earlier instruments? Is an option increase included? What is the priced round price per share? Are conversion discounts present? Does the calculation occur before or after new-money dilution?

**Never promise investors a guaranteed percentage until the executed documents and cap table model support it.**

## 3.5 Dilution waterfall exercise
A company has 8M founders, 2M option reserve and proposes a priced financing that sells 25% post-financing to new investors, before any further changes. The existing 10M fully diluted shares must then be 75% of the total: total post-financing shares = 10M / .75 = **13.333M**; new investor shares ≈ **3.333M**; founders ≈ **60%**, reserve ≈ **15%**, new investor ≈ **25%**. Rounding and real transaction documents can produce other results.

## 3.6 Negotiation and counsel checklist
Ask counsel to explain: conversion events; capitalization definitions; MFN or pro-rata rights if applicable; amendment procedure; information rights; side letters; change-of-control payouts; dissolution priority; interaction with subsequent SAFEs; preferred-stock terms; corporate and securities authorization; tax treatment.

### Examination
**Prompt:** Investor X says a $250k SAFE with a $5M post-money cap buys exactly 5% “forever.”  
**Model answer:** The 5% ratio may approximate one defined pre-round ownership measure, not a perpetually fixed share percentage; future financings can dilute it, and terms/control matter. Request the exact agreement and model multiple scenarios.
