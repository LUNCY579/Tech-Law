## Why a source can be useful without being authenticated as an issuing-court original

A reporter citation and text reproduced by a library or law website may be sufficient to **research** a historical case. Neither a mirror nor an official-looking caption proves that the issuing court's **original** copy was obtained. A later, court-hosted opinion citing the historic case authenticates **the later opinion and its discussion**, not the older opinion's provenance. ## Part I — *Specht*: notice and manifestation of assent

**Procedural posture.** Software users alleged privacy invasion through Netscape's separate SmartDownload plugin. Netscape sought to enforce arbitration clauses. The Southern District of New York denied its motion to compel; the Second Circuit **affirmed** on October 1, 2002 (Nos. **01-7860(L), 01-7870(CON), 01-7872(CON)**).

**What was actually at issue.** Users could activate SmartDownload without receiving reasonably conspicuous notice of its license terms: the terms' existence appeared below the download button and required scrolling. The appellate opinion distinguished these SmartDownload terms from the installation-stage click acceptance of a **separate Netscape Communicator browser**. Do not conflate a Communicator click license with assent to SmartDownload's different contract. The court also examined whether a non-signatory website owner could be estopped from disputing arbitration based on an indirect claimed benefit.

**Narrow result.** On this record, there was no reasonably adequate notice and no unambiguous act of assent to the relevant SmartDownload license; the browser agreement did not automatically cover the plugin dispute; the indirect claimed benefit did not bind the site owner to that arbitration clause. The opinion applied **California contract-law principles** to the notice/assent question. It does not announce that every scrollable, linked or clickwrap online contract is invalid.

**Pinpoint starting points for independent verification:** *Specht*, 306 F.3d **at 20** (summary), **at 28–35** (notice, assent, and related reasoning). The publicly available non-court copy was reviewed for these passages. An actual citation in litigation requires an authenticated reporter/opinion and current authority check.

**Corroboration without overclaiming:** The later **federal court** order in *Small Justice LLC v. Xcentric Ventures LLC*, No. 13-cv-11701, at **7** (D. Mass. Mar. 24, 2014), hosted by GovInfo, quotes and applies *Specht* on whether a reasonably prudent offeree would know of license terms. This confirms a later judicial use of the citation; it is **not** the Second Circuit's original or a complete later-citations search.

**Founder exercise:** A download page features "Get model weights" above a terms link; a different API signup flow forces an unchecked box with a full contract link. Capture both interfaces, distinct product/service terms, when notice appears, actual click events, jurisdiction, alleged user knowledge and available evidence. Do not conclude the interface is enforceable solely from button styling or log existence.

## Part II — *Van Gorkom*: information quality, contract duties and statutory defenses

**Procedural posture.** Shareholders challenged directors' approval of Trans Union's merger process. The Delaware Supreme Court reversed the trial decision and held that on the record the board's process was not an informed exercise of business judgment; the Court found the directors' approval grossly negligent. **Original case:** 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985); majority opinion decided **January 29, 1985**, denial of reargument **March 14, 1985**. A third-party transcription includes both stages. This is **historical case research**, not a certified facsimile.

**Relevant facts:** board approval with compressed deliberation, no adequate valuation documentation, uncertainty about how the proposed price was derived, inadequate informed inquiry and material stockholder disclosure questions. It is too broad to teach that a board must always hire an investment bank or that quick approval **alone** always creates liability. Fact specificity and the full record matter.

**Current statutory qualification:** [8 Del. C. §102(b)(7)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc01/index.html) allows specified certificate provisions limiting covered directors' monetary liability, with statutory **exceptions** (including loyalty breaches, bad faith/intentional misconduct, improper personal benefit, etc.). It does **not** itself erase fiduciary duties, automatically protect every corporation, or categorically immunize officers and directors in every procedural posture. Distinguish the time of the act, certificate text, claim type and available remedies.

### Verified later history: narrowly targeted, not a full citator

The **issuing-court-hosted later** Delaware Court of Chancery opinions give an important nuance:
- *Frederick Hsu Living Trust v. ODN Holding Corp.*, C.A. No. 12108-VCL, **corrected Apr. 25, 2017**, printed **p. 46 n. 34**: explains that *Gantler v. Stephens*, **965 A.2d 695, 713 & n.54 (Del. 2009)**, overruled *Van Gorkom* in part **only as to its loose use of “ratification” for the legally required stockholder vote**, and notes the Delaware Supreme Court's treatment in *Corwin v. KKR Financial Holdings LLC*, **125 A.3d 304, 311 (Del. 2015)**. This is the Chancery judge's explanation of prior Supreme Court precedents, not a new holding overruling them.
- *In re Columbia Pipeline Group, Inc. Merger Litigation*, C.A. No. 2018-0484-JTL (Del. Ch. **May 15, 2024**), printed **p. 40 n. 51**, reiterates that later-history nuance and discusses why fiduciary status does not automatically allow a corporation to escape valid contractual commitments.

**Source proof:** both Delaware Chancery documents are available as [official Delaware Courts PDFs](https://courts.delaware.gov/Opinions/Download.aspx?id=364030) and their relevant pages were checked as page images. This authenticates these **2017/2024 court opinions only**.

**Student exercise:** An unconflicted board votes to sell a private startup after reviewing only a one-page summary, while investors later approve the merger. Analyze: (a) what information was available, (b) notice and directors' care, (c) stockholder disclosure, (d) whether exculpatory certificate text exists, (e) any loyalty/bad-faith allegations, (f) required vs non-required vote distinction, (g) deal-specific law and procedural posture. Do not equate the stockholder vote with automatic immunity.
